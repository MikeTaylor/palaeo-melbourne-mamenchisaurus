Thank you for your enquiry. Apologies it has taken me a while to get back to you but I wanted to give you as much information as I could. 
 
The Firth Dinosaurs exhibition was held at Auckland Museum in 1987, it was on display for 12 weeks, from 15th October 1987 until 5th January 1988. It was discussed in the Auckland Museum Annual Report 1987-88 on pages 17 and 25, this has been digitised and is available via Internet Archive which I have linked. It was also mentioned in several newspaper articles, including  this article in The Press, a Canterbury newspaper which I have also linked. Many other pres clippings remain in copyright and are yet undigitised because of this. Here is a short blurb about the exhibition from the Museum's exhibition history:

> First ever exhibition of dinosaurs held at Auckland Museum and New Zealand. Exhibition was the most popular the Museum had ever held at the time, with approx. 130,000 visitors over the 12 weeks it was on display. Dinosaurs from China, Australia, North America and Europe, and one bone from New Zealand.
 
I have also scanned some of the pages from the booklet you mentioned, this booklet is still in copyright so I have only reproduced limited content, I have included the foreword, page on Mamenchisaurus, and the publication page so you are able to reference the text if you wish in your paper.

I also looked through some Museum Archives material on your behalf and found some additional information. The Mamenchisaurus was a big crowd-pleaser in the exhibition, it was presented in the centre of the room, and had custom-built scaffolding to support the skeleton which the company found one of the most unusual of their commissioned jobs. The Museum of Victoria Curator of Vertebrate Palaeontology at the time was very impressed with the layout of the exhibition in particular the Mamenchisaurus. The specimen was nicknamed Fred by Museum of Victoria staff and would have weighed 40-50 tonnes in its prime. The skeleton was discovered in the Szechuan province in Western China in the 1950s. The whole exhibition was insured for $500,000, a very large figure for the time.
